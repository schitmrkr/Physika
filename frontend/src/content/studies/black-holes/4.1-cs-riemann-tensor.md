### Riemann Tensor

Now let's anlayse the Riemann equation,
$$
R(X,Y) = \nabla_X \nabla_Y - \nabla_Y \nabla_X - \nabla_{[X,Y]}
$$

**Notation:**

$$
Z^j_{;k} = \nabla_k Z^j 
$$

$$
\nabla_Y Z = Y^k \nabla_k Z^j 
$$

$$
\nabla_Y Z = Y^k Z^j_{;k} e_j
$$

Lets analyse the right hand side of equation in local coordinate basis for **vector field**,

$$
\nabla_X \nabla_Y Z = \nabla_X (Y^k Z^j_{;k} e_j) = Y^k \nabla_k (Z^j_{;k} e_j) + Z^j_{;k} e_j \nabla_X Y^k
$$

$$
= Y^k X^l Z^j_{;k;l} e_j + Y^k_{;l} X^l Z^j_{;k} e_j
$$

Accordingly,

$$
\nabla_X \nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y \nabla_X Z = Y^k X^l (Z^j_{;k;l} - Z^j_{;l;k}) e_j + [Y^k_{;l} X^l - X^k_{;l} Y^l] Z^j_{;k} e_j
$$

We have $[Y^k_{;l} X^l - X^k_{;l} Y^l] Z^j_{;k} e_j$ which at first looks unrelated to Lie bracket. But it is almost Lie bracket. Recall:

$$
[X, Y]^k = X^l \partial_l Y^k - Y^l \partial_l X^k
$$

But covariant derivative contains Christofell symbol

$$
Y^k_{;l} = \partial_l Y^k + \Gamma^k_{nl} Y^n
$$

$$
X^k_{;l} = \partial_l X^k + \Gamma^k_{nl} X^n
$$

Thus,

$$
Y^k_{;l} X^l - X^k_{;l} Y^l = [X, Y]^k + X^l Y^n(\Gamma^k_{nl} - \Gamma^k_{ln})
$$

Therefore,


$$
\nabla_X \nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y \nabla_X Z = Y^k X^l (Z^j_{;k;l} - Z^j_{;l;k}) e_j  + [X, Y]^k Z^j_{;k} e_j + X^l Y^n(\Gamma^k_{nl} - \Gamma^k_{ln}) Z^j_{;k} e_j
$$

Recall torsion in coordinate expression,

$$
T^k_{ln} = \Gamma^k_{nl} - \Gamma^k_{ln}
$$

Therefore,

$$
Y^k_{;l} X^l - X^k_{;l} Y^l = [X, Y]^k + T^k_{nl} X^l Y^n
$$

Also,

$$
\nabla_{[X, Y]}Z = [X, Y]^k Z^j_{;k} e_j
$$

Finally,

$$
R(X, Y)Z = (Z^j_{;k;l} - Z^j_{;l;k} + T^n_{kl}Z^j_{;n}) X^l Y^k e_j 
$$

$$
= R^j_{ilk} Z^i X^l Y^k e_j
$$

Thus we obtain the relation,

$$
Z^j_{;k;l} - Z^j_{;l;k} = - R^j_{ikl} Z^i + T^n_{kl} Z^j_{;n} 
$$

This is the Ricci identity.

**Similarly for $1$-form,**

$$
R(X,Y) \Omega = X^l Y^k [\Omega_{j;k;l} - \Omega_{j;l;k} + T^n_{lk} \Omega_{j;n}] e^j
$$

To derive the above:

$$
R(X,Y) = \nabla_X \nabla_Y - \nabla_Y \nabla_X - \nabla_{[X,Y]}
$$

Write $X$, $Y$, and $\Omega$ in a basis

$$
X = X^l e_l, \space Y = Y^l e_l
$$

One form of $\Omega$

$$
\Omega = \Omega_j e^j
$$

where $e^j$ is a dual basis.

$$
e^j(e_k) = \delta^j_k
$$

First, now lets calculate,

$$
\nabla_Y \Omega = Y (\Omega_j)e^j + \Omega_j \nabla_Y e^j
$$

In components, we define

$$
\Omega_{j;k}
$$

such that

$$
\nabla_{e_k} \Omega = \Omega_{j;k} e^j
$$

$$
Y = Y^k e_k
$$

we have,

$$
\nabla_Y \Omega = Y^k \Omega_{j;k} e^j
$$

Now apply $\nabla_X$,

$$
\nabla_X \nabla_Y \Omega = \nabla_X (Y^k \Omega_{j;k} e^j)
$$

$$
= X(Y^k) \Omega_{j;k} e^j + Y^k \nabla_X (\Omega_{j;k} e^j)
$$

Also,

$$
\nabla_X (\Omega_{j;k} e^j) =  X(\Omega_{j;k}) e^j + \Omega_{j;k} \nabla_X e^j
$$

But all of that is exactly what the second covariant derivative represent:

$$
\nabla_X (\nabla_{e_k} \Omega) = X^l \Omega_{j;k;l} e^j
$$

Therefore,

$$
\nabla_X \nabla_Y \Omega = X(Y^k) \Omega_{j;k} e^j + X^l Y^k \Omega_{j;k;l} e^j
$$

Similarly,

$$
\nabla_Y \nabla_X \Omega = Y(X^l) \Omega_{j;l} e^j + Y^k X^l \Omega_{j;l;k} e^j
$$

Now calculate Lie bracket,

$$
[X, Y] = [X^l e_l, Y^k e_k]
$$

In components,

$$
[X,Y] = [X^l Y^k_{,l} - Y^k X^l_{,k}] e_k
$$

Som

$$
\nabla_{[X,Y]} \Omega = [X, Y]^k \Omega_{j;k} e^j
$$

Putting them all together.

$$
R(X,Y) \Omega = X^l Y^k (\Omega_{j;k;l} - \Omega_{j;l;k}) + [X(Y^k) \Omega_{j;k} - Y(X^l) \Omega_{j;l} - [X, Y]^k \Omega_{j;k}] e^j
$$

$$
R(X,Y) \Omega = X^l Y^k (\Omega_{j;k;l} - \Omega_{j;l;k} + T^n_{lk} \Omega_{j;n}) e^j
$$

### Intuition

$$
dV^i = R^i_{j12}\,V^j\,dX^1\,dX^2
$$

describes the change in a vector $V$ after parallel-transporting it around an infinitesimal closed loop in the $X^1-X^2$ plane.

$$
\begin{aligned}
dV^1 &= dX^1\,dX^2\left(V^1 R^1_{112} + V^2 R^1_{212} + V^3 R^1_{312}\right), \\[8pt]
dV^2 &= dX^1\,dX^2\left(V^1 R^2_{112} + V^2 R^2_{212} + V^3 R^2_{312}\right), \\[8pt]
dV^3 &= dX^1\,dX^2\left(V^1 R^3_{112} + V^2 R^3_{212} + V^3 R^3_{312}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$


### Ricci Identity

It is of interest to contrast Ricci identity with the equation we previously derived.

$$
f_{,k;l} - f_{,l;k} = T^n_{kl} f_{,n}, \text{ where } T^n_{kl} = (\Gamma^n_{lk} - \Gamma^n_{kl})
$$

**Proof:**

For a scalar field, the first covariant derivative is simply

$$
f_{;k} = f_{,k}
$$

But its second covariant derivative is

$$
f_{;k;l} = \partial_l f_{,k} - \Gamma^n_{kl} f_{,n}
$$

Similarly,

$$
f_{;l;k} = \partial_k f_{,l} - \Gamma^n_{lk} f_{,n}
$$

Therefore, 

$$
f_{;k;l} - f_{;l;k} = (\Gamma^n_{lk} - \Gamma^n_{kl}) f_{,n}, \text{ where } T^n_{kl} = (\Gamma^n_{lk} - \Gamma^n_{kl})
$$


**A further result of some improtance**

$$
R(X, Y)f = \nabla_X(Y^j f_{,j}) - \nabla_Y{X^j f_{,j}} - [X, Y]^j f_{,j}
$$

$$
= (X^i Y^j_{;l} - Y^l X^j_{;l}) f_{,j} + Y^j X^l (f_{,j;l} - f_{,l;j}) - [X, Y]^j f_,j
$$

$$
= X^l Y^n (\Gamma^j_{nl} - \Gamma^j_{ln}) f_{,j} + X^l Y^j(- \Gamma^n_{jl} + \Gamma^n_{lj})f_{,n} = 0
$$

**Curvature acting on Tensors**

Now we shall consider the effect of $R(X, Y)$ on arbitrary tensor fields. 

$$
R(X, Y)(P \otimes Q) = R(X, Y)P \otimes Q + P \otimes  R(X, Y) Q
$$

where $P$ and $Q$ are arbitrary tensor fields. This is true because the curvature operator is constructed from covariant derivatives. And covariant derivatives follow Liebniz rule.


We can find the effect of $R(X,Y)$ on a one-from $\Omega$. Thus if $Z$ is any contravariant vector-field, it follows

$$
R(X, Y)(\Omega_j Z^j) = [R(X, Y)\Omega]_j Z^j + \Omega_j [R(X, Y)Z]^j
$$

Since, $R(X,Y)$ acting on a scalar field vanishes, we conclude

$$
[R(X,Y) \Omega]_j Z^j = - \Omega_j R^j_{ilk} Z^i X^l Y^k = - \Omega_i R^i_{jlk} Z^j X^l Y^k
$$

Hence,

$$
[R(X,Y) \Omega]_j = - \Omega_i R^i_{jlk} X^l Y^k
$$

On other hand evaluating the effect of $R(X,Y)$ on $\Omega$ by the same procedure that was followed in deriving equation, we obtain

$$
R(X,Y) \Omega = X^l Y^k [\Omega_{j;k;l} - \Omega_{j;l;k} + T^n_{lk} \Omega_{j;n}] e^j
$$

Comparing the above two equations

$$
\Omega_{j;k;l} - \Omega_{j;l;k} = R^i_{jlk} \Omega_i + T^n_{kl} \Omega_{j;n}
$$

Again, considering the effect of $R(X,Y)$ on a tensor of type $(2,0)$, we have

$$
R(X, Y)[S^{ij} e_i \otimes e_j] = R(X, Y)S^{ij} e_i \otimes e_j + S^{ij} R(X, Y) e_i \otimes e_j + S^{ij} e_i \otimes R(X, Y) e_j \\
=(R^i_{lmn} S^{lj} + R^j_{lmn} S^{il}) X^m Y^n e_i \otimes e_j
$$

because

$$
R(X, Y)S^{ij} = 0
$$

and we conclude that

$$
S^{ij}_{;k;l} - S^{ij}_{;l;k} = - R^i_{mkl} S^{mj} - R^j_{mkl} S^{mj} + T^n_{kl} S^{ij}_{;n}
$$

In similar fashion, we find

$$
S_{ij;k;l} - S_{ij;l;k} = R^m_{jkl} S_{im} + R^m_{ikl} S_{mj} + T^n_{kl} S_{ij;n}
$$

For a torsion-free connection, this simplifies to

$$
S^{ij}_{;k;l} - S^{ij}_{;l;k} = - R^i_{mkl} S^{mj} - R^j_{mkl} S^{mj}
$$

$$
S_{ij;k;l} - S_{ij;l;k} = R^m_{jkl} S_{im} + R^m_{ikl} S_{mj}
$$


We note that by contracting the Riemann tensor with respect to the second(or third) covariant index, we obtain the Ricci tensor(or its negative); thus

$$
R^j_{ljm} = - R^j_{lmj} =  R_{lm}
$$

**In local coordinate basis,**

$$
R^i_{jkl} = \partial_k \Gamma^i_{jl} - \partial_l \Gamma^i_{jk} + \Gamma^i_{mk} \Gamma^m_{jl} - \Gamma^i_{ml} \Gamma^m_{jk}
$$

This can be derived using the curvature operator:

$$
R(X,Y) = \nabla_X \nabla_Y - \nabla_Y \nabla_X - \nabla_{[X,Y]}
$$

After contracting  first and third index we get the Ricci tensor,

$$
R_{jl} = R^i_{jil} = \partial_i \Gamma^i_{jl}
- \partial_l \Gamma^i_{ji}
+ \Gamma^i_{ki}\Gamma^k_{jl}
- \Gamma^i_{kl}\Gamma^k_{ji}
$$
