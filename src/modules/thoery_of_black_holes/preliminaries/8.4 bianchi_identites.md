### The Bianchi identities

As we have stated earlier , there are, altogether, twenty linearly-independent Bianchi identities. A complete set is provided by the eight complex identities

$$
R_{13[13|4]} = 0; \space R_{13[21|4]} = 0; \space R_{13[13|2]} = 0; \space R_{13[43|2]} = 0; \space \\
R_{42[13|4]} = 0; \space R_{42[21|4]} = 0; \space R_{42[13|2]} = 0; \space R_{42[43|2]} = 0; 
$$

and the four real identites which follow from

$$
\eta^{bc} (R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}R)_{|c} = 0
$$

Explicitly written out, the above equation provides the two real equations.

$$
R_{11|2} + R_{34|1} - R_{13|4} - R_{14|3} = 0 \\
R_{22|1} + R_{34|2} - R_{23|4} - R_{24|3} = 0 
$$

and one complex equation

$$
R_{33|4} + R_{12|3} - R_{31|2} - R_{32|1} = 0 
$$

We shall write down the explict forms of the various identities in terms of the spin coefficients and the Weyl and the Ricci scalars. As an example consider the first of the identities listed

$$
R_{1313|4} + R_{1334|1} + R_{1341|3} = 0
$$

By making use of relations of Riemann with Weyl, Ricci

$$
C_{1313|4} + (C_{1334} + \frac{1}{2} R_{13})_{|1} - \frac{1}{2} R_{11|3} = 0
$$

Considering the terms in the Weyl tensor, we have

$$
C_{1313|4} = C_{1313,4} - \eta^{nm} (\gamma_{n14} C_{m313} + \gamma_{n34}C_{1m13} \\
+ \gamma_{n14} C_{13m3} + \gamma_{n34} C_{131m}) \\
= C_{1313,4} - 2(\gamma_{214} + \gamma_{344})C_{1313} + 2 \gamma_{314} (C_{1213} + C_{4313}) \\
 = - \delta^* \Psi_0 + 4 \alpha \Psi_0 - 4 \rho \Psi_1
$$

and 

$$
C_{1334|1} = C_{1334,1} + \eta^{nm} [\gamma_{n11} C_{m334} + \gamma_{n31} (C_{1m34} + C_{13m4}) \\
+ \gamma_{n41} C_{133m}] \\
= C_{1334,1} + [(\gamma_{211} + \gamma_{341})C_{1334} + \gamma_{131}(C_{1234} - C_{3434}) \\
+ \gamma_{231} C_{1314} + \gamma_{141} C_{1332} + \gamma_{131} C_{1324} + \gamma_{241} C_{1331}] \\
= D \Psi_1 - 2 \epsilon \Psi_1 + 3 \kappa \Psi_2 - \pi \Psi_0
$$

Similarly, we find

$$
\frac{1}{2}(R_{13|1} - R_{11|3}) = - D \Phi_{01} + \delta \Phi_{00} + 2(\epsilon + \rho^*) \Phi_{01} + 2 \sigma \Phi_{10}\\
- 2 \kappa \Phi_{11} - \kappa^* \Phi_{02} + (\pi^* - 2 \alpha^* - 2 \beta) \Phi_{00}
$$

The remaining identites can be derived in similar fashion.

**Notes**

For the Riemann tensor,

$$
R_{ab[cd|e]} = 0
$$

Here the bracket means antisymmetrization over $a, b, c$. Explicitly,

$$
R_{abcd|e} + R_{abde|c} + R_{abec|d} = 0
$$

The Bianchi identities impose relations among its derivatives. When expressed in the complex NP tetrad, a convenient complete set can be represented by $8$ complex identities and $4$ real identities. 

Start with a covector $V_a$,

$$
[\nabla_c, \nabla_d] V_a = \nabla_c \nabla_d V_a - \nabla_d \nabla_c V_a = - R^b_{acd} V_b
$$

$$
R_{abcd} = g_{ae} R^e_{bcd}
$$

The Bianchi identities is due to the fact that covariant derivatives satisfy a Jacobi identity 

$$
[A, [B, C]] + [B, [C, A]] + [C, [A, B]] = 0
$$

A complete set of Bianchi identity

$$
R_{13[13|4]} = 0; \space R_{13[21|4]} = 0; \space R_{13[13|2]} = 0; \space R_{13[43|2]} = 0; \space \\
R_{42[13|4]} = 0; \space R_{42[21|4]} = 0; \space R_{42[13|2]} = 0; \space R_{42[43|2]} = 0; 
$$

Now, 

$$
R_{[ab][cd|e]}
$$

There are 6 independent pairs in $[ab]$, 

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
4 \\
2
\end{pmatrix} = 6
$$

For each fixed $ab$, you might initially imagine choosing combinations of $cde$, but there are only 

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
4 \\
3
\end{pmatrix} = 4
$$

independent choices of three directions

$$
123, 124, 134, 234
$$

There are therefore $6 \times 4 = 24$ raw looking combinations.

Now why do we choose $13$ and $42$?

These are useful because they select two complex-conjugate sectors of the curvature.

$$
13 = (l, m)
$$

$$
42 = (\bar{m}, n)
$$

These are essentially the two sides of the null tetrad structure.

The eight equations are

$$
\boxed{\text{4 equations with 13 + 4 equations with 42}}
$$

This gives $8$ complex equations. Their complex conjugates supply another eight real equations.

Another $4$ real equations are given by contracted Bianchi identity.

$$
\eta^{bc} (R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}R)_{|c} = 0
$$

To prove this, we start with the differential Bianchi identity

$$
\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho \sigma \mu \nu} + \nabla_\mu R_{\rho \sigma \nu \lambda} + \nabla_\nu R_{\rho \sigma \lambda \mu} = 0
$$

or

$$
R_{\rho [\sigma \mu \nu | \lambda]} = 0
$$

Contract $\rho$ with $\mu$,

$$
g^{\rho \mu} (\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho \sigma \mu \nu} + \nabla_\mu R_{\rho \sigma \nu \lambda} + \nabla_\nu R_{\rho \sigma \lambda \mu}) = 0
$$

Because of metric compatibility,

$$
\nabla_\lambda g^{\rho \mu} = 0
$$

Therefore,

$$
\nabla_\lambda (g^{\rho \mu} R_{\rho \sigma \mu \nu}) + \nabla_\mu (g^{\rho \mu} R_{\rho \sigma \nu \lambda}) + \nabla_\nu (g^{\rho \mu} R_{\rho \sigma \lambda \mu}) = 0
$$

$$
\nabla_\lambda R_{\sigma \nu} + \nabla_\mu R^\mu_{\sigma \nu \lambda} - \nabla_\nu R_{\sigma \lambda} = 0
$$

Now if we contract again, we get contracted Bianchi identity,

$$
\nabla^\nu R_{\mu \nu} = \frac{1}{2} \nabla_\mu R
$$

Now we define Einstein tensor,

$$
G_{ab} = R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2} g_{ab} R
$$

Take its divergence,

$$
\nabla^b G_{ab} = \nabla^b R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2} \nabla_b (g_{ab} R) =  \nabla^b R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2} g_{ab} \nabla^b R
$$

$$
\nabla^b G_{ab} = \nabla^b R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2} \nabla_a R = 0
$$

Therefore,

$$
\nabla^b G_{ab} = \nabla^b (R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2} g_{ab} R) = 0
$$

This can be written as

$$
\eta^{bc} (R_{ab} - \frac{1}{2}\eta_{ab}R)_{|c} = 0
$$

The above equation provides the two real equations,

$$
R_{11|2} + R_{34|1} - R_{13|4} - R_{14|3} = 0 \\
R_{22|1} + R_{34|2} - R_{23|4} - R_{24|3} = 0 
$$

and one complex equation,

$$
R_{33|4} + R_{12|3} - R_{31|2} - R_{32|1} = 0 
$$
