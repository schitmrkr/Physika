### The Ricci and the Bianchi identities

Projecting the Ricci identity

$$
R^i_{jkl} Z_i = Z_{j;k;l} - Z_{j;l;k}
$$

Now let $Z_i = e_{(a)i}$,

$$
e_{(a)i;k;l} - e_{(a)i;l;k} = R_{mikl}e_{(a)}^m
$$

on to the tetrad frame, we have

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = R_{mikl} e^m_{(a)} e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)} \\
= \{ - [\gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_k]_{;l} + [\gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_l]_{;k} \} e^i_{(b)} e^{(k)}_{(c)} e^l_{(d)}
$$

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = - \gamma_{(a)(b)(c),(d)} + \gamma_{(a)(b)(d),(c)}\\ + \gamma_{(b)(a)(f)} [\gamma^{(f)}_{(c)(d)} - \gamma^{(f)}_{(d)(c)}] \\
+ \gamma_{(f)(a)(c)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(d)} - \gamma_{(f)(a)(d)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(c)}
$$

Because of the antisymmetry of the rotation coefficients in the first pair of their indices and the manifest antisymmetry of the operation by which the tetrad components of the Riemann tensor are constructed from them, it is clear that there are 36 equations of the kind.

Finally, the Bianchi identity,

$$
R_{ij[kl;m]} = \frac{1}{3} (R_{ijkl;m} + R_{ijlm;k} + R_{ijmk;l}) = 0
$$

expressed in terms of intrinsic derivatives and tetrad components, takes the form

$$
R_{(a)(b)[(c)(d)|(f)]} = \frac{1}{6} \sum_{[(c)(d)(f)]} \{ R_{(a)(b)(c)(d),(f)} \\
- \eta^{(n)(m)} [\gamma_{(n)(a)(f)} R_{(m)(b)(c)(d)} + \gamma_{(n)(b)(f)} R_{(a)(m)(c)(d)}] \\
+ \gamma_{(n)(c)(f)} R_{(a)(b)(m)(d)} + \gamma_{(n)(d)(f) R_{(a)(b)(c)(m)}} \}
$$

and as noted earlier, there are 20 linearly-independent equations of this kind.

The basic equation of tetrad formalism are the 24 commutation relations, the 36 Ricci identities and 20 linearly independent Bianchi identities.

**Notes**

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = R_{mikl} e^m_{(a)} e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)}
$$

We have,

$$
e_{(a)i;k;l} - e_{(a)i;l;k} = R_{mikl} e^m_{(a)}
$$

Substitute,

$$
e_{(a)i;k} = - \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_k
$$

Then,

$$
e_{(a)i;k;l} = [- \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_k]_{;l}
$$

$$
e_{(a)i;l;k} = [- \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_l]_{;k}
$$

Therefore,

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = \{ - [\gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_k]_{;l} + [\gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_l]_{;k} \} e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)}
$$

Now lets take a look at the first term

$$
- [\gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_k]_{;l} e^i_{(b)} e^{(k)}_{(c)} e^l_{(d)} = [- \gamma_{(a)(f)(g);l} e^{(f)}_i e^{(g)}_k \\
- \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_{i;l} e^{(g)}_{k} - \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_{i} e^{(g)}_{k;l}] e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)}
$$

$$
= - \gamma_{(a)(f)(g);l} \delta^{(f)}_{(b)} \delta^{(g)}_{(c)} e^l_{(d)} - \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(m)(n)} e^{(m)}_i e^{(n)}_l e^{(g)}_{k} e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)} \\
- \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} e^{(f)}_{i} \gamma^{(g)}_{(m)(n)} e^{(m)}_k e^{(n)}_l e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)} 
$$

$$
= - \gamma_{(a)(b)(c),(d)} - \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(m)(n)} \delta^{(m)}_{(b)} \delta^{(n)}_{(d)} \delta^{(g)}_{(c)}\\
- \gamma_{(a)(f)(g)} \gamma^{(g)}_{(m)(n)} \delta^{(m)}_{(c)} \delta^{(f)}_{(b)} \delta^{(n)}_{(d)}
$$

Using the antisymmetry of $\gamma$ we can conclude,

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = - \gamma_{(a)(b)(c),(d)} + \gamma_{(a)(b)(d),(c)}\\ + \gamma_{(b)(a)(f)} [\gamma^{(f)}_{(c)(d)} - \gamma^{(f)}_{(d)(c)}] \\
+ \gamma_{(f)(a)(c)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(d)} - \gamma_{(f)(a)(d)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(c)}
$$
