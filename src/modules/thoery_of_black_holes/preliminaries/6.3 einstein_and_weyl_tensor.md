### Einstein And Weyl Tensor

#### Einstein Tensor

The Einstein tensor $G_{ij}$ is related to the Ricci tensor by

$$
G_{ij} = R_{ij} - \frac{1}{2} g_{ij} R
$$

where

$$
R = R^j_j = g^{ij} R_{ij}
$$

is the contracted Ricci tensor.

The most important property of the Einstein tensor is that its covariant divergence vanishes:

$$
G^i_{j;i} = 0
$$

This identity follows from the Bianci identity. Thus, contracting the relation

$$
R^i_{jkl;m} - g^{ip} R_{jplm;k} + R^i_{jmk;l} = 0
$$

with respect to $i$ and $k$, we obtain

$$
R_{jl;m} - g^{kp} R_{jplm;k} - R_{jm;l} = 0
$$

Now raising the index $j$ and contracting it with $m$, we obtain

$$
R^j_{l;j} + R^k_{l;k} - R_{,l} = 0
$$

$$
(R^j_l - \frac{1}{2} \delta^j_l R)_{;j} = 0
$$

which is the required identity.

#### Weyl Tensor

$$
C_{ijkl} = R_{ijkl} - \frac{1}{(n-2)} (g_{ik}R_{jl} + g_{jl} R_{ik} - g_{jk} R_{il} - g_{il} R_{jk}) \\
+ \frac{1}{(n-1)(n-2)} (g_{ik} g_{jl} - g_{il} g_{jk})R
$$

this tensor has manifestly all the symmetries of the Riemann tensor; but

$$
g^{jl} C_{ijkl} = 0
$$

**Notes**

$$
R_{jl;m} - g^{kp} R_{jplm;k} - R_{jm;l} = 0
$$

Raise index $j$ and contract with $m$,

$$
g^{jq} (R_{jl;m} - g^{kp} R_{jplm;k} - R_{jm;l}) = 0
$$

$$
g^{jm} R_{jl;m} - g^{jm} g^{kp} R_{jplm;k} - g^{jm} R_{jm;l} = 0
$$

Since,

$$
g^{jm}_{;m} = 0
$$

We have,

$$
(g^{jm} R_{jl})_{;m} - (g^{jm} g^{kp} R_{jplm})_{;k} - (g^{jm} R_{jm})_{;l} = 0
$$

Now recall Ricci tensor defintion to evaluate $g^{jm} g^{kp} R_{jplm}$

$$
R_{pl} = g^{jm} R_{jpml}
$$

But our expression has $R_{jplm}$

$$
R_{jplm} = - R_{jpml} \\ 
g^{jm} R_{jplm} = - g^{jm} R_{jpml} \\ 
g^{jm} R_{jplm} = - R_{pl} \\ 
$$

Therefore,

$$
(g^{jm} R_{jl})_{;m} + (g^{kp} R_{pl})_{;k} - (g^{jm} R_{jm})_{;l} = 0
$$

$$
R^m_{l;m} + R^k_{l;k} - R_{,l} = 0
$$

$$
R^j_{l;j} - \frac{1}{2}R_{,l} = 0
$$

This is contracted Bianchi identity. This is precisely what is needed to be shown.

$$
G^j_{l;j} = 0
$$

Then,

$$
G^j_l = R^j_{l} - \frac{1}{2} \delta^j_l R
$$
