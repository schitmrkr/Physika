### Lie bracket and Lie differentiation

Given any two vector fields, $X$ and $Y$, their Lie bracket, $[X, Y]$, is defined by its action on any functions $f$; and it is given by

$$
[X, Y]f = (XY - YX)f = X(Yf) - Y(Xf)
$$


For example, in ordinary two dimensional space

$$
X = X^x \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + X^y \frac{\partial}{\partial y}
$$

$$
Y = Y^x \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + Y^y \frac{\partial}{\partial y}
$$

$$
Xf = X^x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + X^y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
$$

Therefore,

$$
Y(Xf) = Y^x \frac{\partial}{\partial x} (X^x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + X^y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}) + Y^y \frac{\partial}{\partial y} (X^x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + X^y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y})
$$

$$
X(Yf) = X^x \frac{\partial}{\partial x}
\left(
Y^x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
+
Y^y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
\right)
+
X^y \frac{\partial}{\partial y}
\left(
Y^x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
+
Y^y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
\right)
$$

$$
[X, Y]f = X(Yf) - Y(Xf)
$$

The Lie bracket of any two tangent vectors is, again, a tangent vector, since

$$
[X, Y](\alpha f + \beta g) = \alpha [X, Y]f + \beta[X, Y]g
$$

and,

$$
[X, Y](fg) = g[X, Y]f + f[X, Y]g
$$

The third relation follows from the first two.

The Lie bracket satisfies the Jacobi identity,

$$
[[X, Y], Z] + [[Y, Z], X] + [[Z, X], Y] = 0
$$

We know that the Lie bracket of $X, Y$ is a tangent vector. Its components, relative to a local coordinate basis, can be obtained by its action on $x^j$. Thus,

$$
[X, Y]^j = (XY - YX) x^j = X Y^j - Y X^j \\
= X^k Y^j_{,k} - Y^kX^j_{,k}
$$

where a comma preceding an index denotes partial differentiation with respect to the local coordinate with that same index.

In local coordinate basis, the Lie bracket $[\partial_k, \partial_j]$ clearly vanishes.

Considered as a differentiation, $[X, Y]$ is called the lie derivative of $Y$ in the direction $X$ and is written as 

$$
\mathscr{L}_X Y = [X, Y] = -[Y, X] = \mathscr{L}_Y X
$$

More generally, we define the Lie derivative, $\mathscr{L}_X T$, of a tensor field $T$ of a given type, as a tensor of the same type which satisfies the following rules:

**a**. Its action on a scalar field $f$ is given by

$$
\mathscr{L}_X f = Xf = df(X)
$$

**b**. Its action on a tangent vector Y, as we have already defined, is given by 

$$
\mathscr{L}_X Y = [X, Y] 
$$

**c**. It operates linearly on tensor fields and satisfies the Liebnitz rule when acting on tensor products:

$$
\mathscr{L}_X (S \otimes T) = \mathscr{L}_X S \otimes T + S \otimes \mathscr{L}_X T
$$

where S and T are arbitrary tensor fields.


The last of the foregoing rules enables us to derive the effect of $\mathscr{L}_X$ on a tensor of arbitrary type. Thus, its effect on a one-form $\omega$ can be determined by considering, for any arbitrary vector-field $Y$, the contracted version of the realtion

$$
\mathscr{L}_X(\omega \otimes Y) = (\mathscr{L}_X \omega) \otimes Y + \omega \otimes (\mathscr{L}_X Y)
$$

namely,

$$
\mathscr{L}_X \langle \omega, Y \rangle = \langle \mathscr{L}_X \omega , Y \rangle + \langle \omega, \mathscr{L}_X Y \rangle
$$

$$
X^k (\omega_j Y^j)_{,k} = (\mathscr{L}_X \omega)_j Y^j + \omega_j (\mathscr{L}_X Y)^j
$$

Evaluating each term

$$
(\mathscr{L}_X \omega)_j Y^j = X^k (\omega_{j,k} Y^j + \omega_j Y^j_{,k}) - \omega_j(X^kY^j_{,k}-Y^kX^j_{,k})
$$

$$
= (X^k \omega_{j,k} + \omega_k X^k_{,j})Y^j
$$

Therefore,

$$
(\mathscr{L}_X \omega)_j = (X^k \omega_{j,k} + \omega_k X^k_{,j})
$$

**Notes**

How do we differentiate a vector field?

For a scalar function $f(x, y)$, we know how to differentiate it

$$
Xf = X^x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + X^y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
$$

$Xf$ tells us how $f$ changes as we move in the direction of $X$.

Now suppose $Y$ is a vector field. 

$$
Y = Y^x \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + Y^y \frac{\partial}{\partial y}
$$

How does $Y$ change as we move in the direction of $X$?

This is the geometric motivation for Lie derivatives.

$$
\mathscr{L}_X(Y) = [X, Y]
$$

The Lie bracket measures the infinitesimal failure of the two flows to commute.
