### The Ricci Identities and the eliminant relations (Ctnd.)

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = - \gamma_{(a)(b)(c),(d)} + \gamma_{(a)(b)(d),(c)}\\ + \gamma_{(b)(a)(f)} [\gamma^{(f)}_{(c)(d)} - \gamma^{(f)}_{(d)(c)}] \\
+ \gamma_{(f)(a)(c)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(d)} - \gamma_{(f)(a)(d)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(c)}
$$

While each of the foregoing equations contains one or more of the Weyl and the Ricci scalars, it is clear that one should be able to obtain from them total of sixteen real equations which are independent of them and involve only the spin coefficients. The reason is that , while the right-hand side of the equation above is antisymmetric in first pair and in second pair of indices, it has not the invariance of teh Riamann tensor for simultaneous interchange of the first and the second index of pairs. It is precisely this latter invariance (together with the cyclic identity) which reduces the number of independent components of the Riemann tensor from $36$ to $20$.The elimination can be carried out directly from the equations listed. By systematic eliminations, we obtain the following set of four real and six complex eliminant relations involving only the spin coefficient:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(\rho-\rho^*)
+\delta\kappa^*
-\delta^*\kappa
={}&
(\rho-\rho^*)
(\rho+\rho^*+\epsilon+\epsilon^*) \\
&+\kappa(\tau^*+\pi-3\alpha-\beta^*)
-\kappa^*(\tau+\pi^*-3\alpha^*-\beta).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(\mu-\mu^*)
+\delta(\alpha+\beta^*-\pi)
-\delta^*(\alpha^*+\beta-\pi^*)
={}&
(\gamma+\gamma^*)(\rho-\rho^*) \\
&+\alpha(\pi^*-2\beta)
-\alpha^*(\pi-2\beta^*) \\
&+\kappa^*\nu^*
-\kappa\nu
+\beta\pi-\beta^*\pi^* \\
&+(\rho+\rho^*)(\mu-\mu^*).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(\mu-\mu^*-\gamma+\gamma^*)
+\Delta(\epsilon-\epsilon^*)
-\delta\pi+\delta^*\pi^*
={}&
(\epsilon+\epsilon^*)(\mu^*-\mu) \\
&+\tau^*(\alpha^*+\pi^*-\beta)
-\tau(\alpha+\pi-\beta^*) \\
&+\lambda\sigma-\lambda^*\sigma^*
+\rho^*\mu-\rho\mu^* \\
&+2(\epsilon\gamma-\epsilon^*\gamma^*).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(\mu^*-\mu)
+\delta\nu-\delta^*\nu^*
={}&
(\mu-\mu^*)
(\mu+\mu^*+\gamma+\gamma^*) \\
&+\nu(\tau-3\beta-\alpha^*+\pi^*) \\
&-\nu^*(\tau^*+\pi-3\beta^*-\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(\tau-\alpha^*-\beta)
-\Delta\kappa
+\delta(\epsilon+\epsilon^*)
={}&
\rho(\tau+\pi^*)
+\kappa^*\lambda^*
+\sigma(\tau^*-\alpha-\beta^*) \\
&+\epsilon(\tau-\pi^*)
-\rho^*(\beta+\alpha^*+\pi^*) \\
&+\epsilon^*(2\alpha^*+2\beta-\tau-\pi^*) \\
&+\kappa(\mu-2\gamma).
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta(\rho-\epsilon+\epsilon^*)
-\delta^*\sigma
+D(\beta-\alpha^*)
={}&
\rho(\alpha^*+\beta+\tau) \\
&-\rho^*(\tau-\beta+\alpha^*+\pi^*) \\
&+(\epsilon^*-\epsilon)(2\alpha^*-\pi^*) \\
&+\sigma(\pi-2\alpha)
+\kappa(\gamma^*-\gamma-\mu^*)
+\kappa^*\lambda^*.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D\lambda+\Delta\sigma^*
-\delta^*(\tau^*+\pi)
={}&
\sigma^*(3\gamma^*-\gamma+\mu-\mu^*) \\
&+(\pi+\tau^*)(\pi-\tau^*+\alpha) \\
&+\lambda(\rho-\rho^*-3\epsilon+\epsilon^*) \\
&-\beta\pi-\tau^*\beta^*.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D\nu
+\Delta(\alpha+\beta^*-\pi)
-\delta^*(\gamma+\gamma^*)
={}&
\nu(\rho-2\epsilon)
+\lambda(\pi^*-\alpha^*-\beta) \\
&+\mu(\pi+\tau^*)
-\mu^*(\alpha+\beta^*+\tau^*) \\
&+\gamma(\pi-\tau^*) \\
&+\gamma^*(2\alpha+2\beta^*-\pi-\tau^*)
+\sigma^*\nu^*.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(\beta^*-\alpha)
+\delta\lambda
+\delta^*(\gamma-\gamma^*-\mu)
={}&
\nu(\epsilon^*-\epsilon-\rho^*) \\
&+\lambda(\tau-2\beta)
+\alpha(\mu+\mu^*) \\
&-\mu^*(\pi+\tau^*+\beta^*)
+\mu(\pi+\beta^*) \\
&+(\gamma-\gamma^*)(\tau^*-2\beta^*)
+\sigma^*\nu^*.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D\mu+\Delta\rho-\delta\pi-\delta^*\tau
={}&
\rho^*\mu-\rho\mu^*
+\pi(\pi^*-\alpha^*+\beta) \\
&+\tau(\beta^*-\alpha-\tau^*)
+\rho(\gamma+\gamma^*) \\
&-\mu(\epsilon+\epsilon^*).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Notes**

$$
R_{abcd}
$$

In four dimensions, each antisymmetric pair of indices has 

$$
\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = 6
$$

possibilities. So, it can be though of as $6 \times 6$ matrix, which means it has 36 independent components.

But Riemann has another additional symmetry.

$$
R_{abcd} = R_{cdab}
$$

A $6 \times 6$ symmetric matrix has 

$$
6(7)/2 = 21
$$ 

independent components. Then, the cyclic identity 

$$
R_{abcd} + R_{acdb} + R_{adbc} = 0
$$

removes one more independent component making the independent components $20$. Therefore, $36 - 20 = 16$ relations among the 36 equations do not involve curvature independently.
