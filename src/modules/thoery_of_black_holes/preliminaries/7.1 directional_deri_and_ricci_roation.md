### Directional derivatives and Ricci rotation-coefficient

The contravariant vectors $e_{(a)}$, considered as tangent vectors, define the directional derivatives

$$
e_{(a)} = e^i_{(a)} \frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}
$$

and we shall write

$$
\phi_{,(a)} = e^i_{(a)} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x^i} = e^i_{(a)} \phi_{,i}
$$

More generally we define,

$$
A_{(a),(b)} = e^i_{(b)}  \frac{\partial A_{(a)}}{\partial x^i} = e^i_{(b)}  \frac{\partial}{\partial x^i} e^j_{(a)} A_j
$$

$$
= e^i_{(b)} \nabla_{\partial_i} [e^j_{(a)} A_j] = e^i_{(b)} [e^j_{(a)} A_{j;i} + A_k e^k_{(a);i}]
$$

Therefore,

$$
A_{(a),(b)} =  e^j_{(a)} A_{j;i} e^i_{(b)} + e_{(a)k;i} e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} A^{(c)} 
$$

With the definition

$$
\gamma_{(c)(a)(b)} = e_{(a)k;i} e^i_{(b)} e^k_{(c)}
$$

Thus the equation becomes,

$$
A_{(a),(b)} =  e^j_{(a)} A_{j;i} e^i_{(b)} + \gamma_{(c)(a)(b)} A^{(c)} 
$$

The quantities $\gamma_{(c)(a)(b)}$ are called the Ricci rotation-coefficients. An equivalent definition of these coeeficient is 

$$
e_{(a)k;i} = e^{(c)}_{k} \gamma_{(c)(a)(b)} e^{(b)}_{i}
$$

The Ricci rotation coefficients are antisymmetric in the first pair of indices;

$$
\gamma_{(c)(a)(b)} = - \gamma_{(a)(c)(b)}
$$

a fact which follows from expanding the identity

$$
0 = \eta_{(a)(b), i} = [e_{(a)k} e^{k}_{(b)}]_{;i}
$$

By the virtue of this assymetry, we can also write

$$
e^k_{(a);i} = - \gamma^k_{(a)i}
$$

Returning to equation $A_{(a),(b)} =  e^j_{(a)} A_{j;i} e^i_{(b)} + \gamma_{(c)(a)(b)} A^{(c)} $, we write it in alternative form

$$
 e^i_{(a)} A_{i;j} e^j_{(b)} = A_{(a),(b)} - \eta^{(n)(m)} \gamma_{(n)(a)(b)} A_{(m)} 
$$

The quantity on the right side of this equation is called the intrinsic derivative of $A_{(a)}$ in the direction of $e_{(b)}$ and written $A_{(a)|(b)}$

$$
A_{(a)|(b)} = e^i_{(a)} A_{i;j} e^j_{(b)}
$$

or

$$
A_{i;j} = e^{(a)}_i A_{(a)|(b)} e^{(b)}_j
$$

The intrinsic derivative of the Riemann tensor is given by

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)|(f)} = R_{ijkl;m} e^i_{(a)} e^j_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)} e^m_{(f)}
$$

Now expanding 

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d),(f)} = [R_{ijkl} e^i_{(a)} e^j_{(b)} e^k_{(c)} e^l_{(d)}]_{;m} e^m_{(f)}
$$

replacing the covariant derivatives of the different basis vectors by the respectibve roatiation coefficient we find

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)|(f)} = R_{(a)(b)(c)(d),(f)} \\
- \eta^{(n)(m)} [\gamma_{(n)(a)(f)} R_{(m)(b)(c)(d)} + \gamma_{(n)(b)(f)} R_{(a)(m)(c)(d)}\\
 + \gamma_{(n)(c)(f)} R_{(a)(b)(m)(d)} + \gamma_{(n)(d)(f)} R_{(a)(b)(c)(m)}]
$$

It is important ot observee that the evaluation of the rotation coefficients does not require the evaluation of covariant derivatives. For, defining

$$
\lambda_{(a)(b)(c)} = e_{(b)i,j} [e^i_{(a)} e^j_{(c)} - e^j_{(a)} e^i_{(c)}]
$$

and rewriting in the form 

$$
\lambda_{(a)(b)(c)} = [e_{(b)i,j} - e_{(b)j,i}] e^i_{(a)} e^j_{(c)}
$$

we observe that we can replace the ordinary derivatives of $e_{(b)i}$ and $e_{(b)j}$ by the corresponding covariant derivatives

$$
\lambda_{(a)(b)(c)} = [e_{(b)i;j} - e_{(b)j;i}] e^i_{(a)} e^j_{(c)} \\
= \gamma_{(a)(b)(c)} - \gamma_{(c)(b)(a)}
$$

By virtue of this relation, we have

$$
\gamma_{(a)(b)(c)} = \frac{1}{2} [\lambda_{(a)(b)(c)} + \lambda_{(c)(a)(b)} - \lambda_{(b)(c)(a)}]
$$

Notice that the $\lambda$ symbols are antisymmetric in the first and the third indices:

$$
\lambda_{(a)(b)(c)} = - \lambda_{(c)(b)(a)}
$$
