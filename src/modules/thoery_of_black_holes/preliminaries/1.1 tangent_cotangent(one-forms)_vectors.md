### Tangent Vectors

With the definition of $f(x^1(t), ..., x^n(t))$ on a curve $\lambda$ on $M$, which we have just given, consider

$$
(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t})_{\lambda(t)} |_{t = t_0} = \lim_{\epsilon \rightarrow 0} \frac{1}{\epsilon} \left\{ f(\lambda(t_0 + \epsilon)) - f(\lambda(t_0)) \right\}
$$

$$
= \sum^n_{j=1} \frac{dx^j(t)}{dt} |_{t_0} (\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j})_{\lambda(t_0)} = (\frac{dx^j}{dt} \frac{df}{dx^j})_{t_0}
$$

Note: Einstein summantion over repeated indices is assumed.

We can define a linear vector space at $p$ consisting of linear combinations of the coordinate derivatives $\partial/\partial x^j$ of forms

$$
X = X^j \frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}
$$

where $X^j$ are any set of $n$ numbers. These tangent vectors arise by considering the curves $\lambda$ defined by

$$
x^j(t) = x^j(p) + X^jt
$$

for $t$ in some small interval $-\epsilon < t < +\epsilon$.

The tangent vectors at $p$ form a linear vector space over $\mathcal{R}^1$ spanned by the coordinate derivatives, since the requirement for a linear vector space, namely,

$$
(\alpha X + \beta Y)f = \alpha(Xf) + \beta(Yf)
$$

Finally by definition of $X$ by

$$
Xf =  X^j \frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j} = X^jf_{,j}
$$

for every smooth function $f$ clearly satisfies the Leibnitz rule when operating on products of functions, thus

$$
X(fg)|_{\lambda(t)} = (fXg+ gXf)|_{\lambda(t)}
$$

Instead of basis determined by local coordinates, we may choose any oter $n$ linearly independent vectors $e_a(a= 1,..,n)$. Then there must exist linear relations of form

$$
\mathbf{e}_a = \Phi_a^k \frac{\partial}{\partial x^k}
$$

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}  = \Phi_j^b \mathbf{e}_b
$$

where $[ \Phi_a^k]$ and $[\Phi_j^b]$  are inverses.

Given any basis, we can epxress any tangent vector at $p$ in the form,

$$
X = X^j e_j
$$


### One forms (or cotangent or covariant vectors)

A one-form, $\omega$, at $p$ is a linear mapping of the tangent space $T_p$ on to the reals:

$$
\omega: T_p \to \mathcal{R}^1
$$

Given any tangent vector $X$ at $p$, the one-form associates uniquely with a number $\omega(X)$ which is alos written as

$$
\omega(X) = \langle \omega, X \rangle
$$

The required linearity of the map is expressed by the relation

$$
\langle \omega, \alpha X + \beta Y\rangle = \alpha \langle \omega, X \rangle + \beta \langle \omega, Y \rangle
$$

$$
(\alpha \omega)(X) = \alpha \langle \omega, X \rangle
$$

$$
(\omega + \pi)(X) =  \langle \omega, X \rangle + \langle \pi, X \rangle
$$

One forms span a vector space which we denote by $T^*_p$.
