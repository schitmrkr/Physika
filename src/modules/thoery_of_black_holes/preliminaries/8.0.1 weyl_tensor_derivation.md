### Weyl Tensor 

$$
C_{abcd} = R_{abcd} - \cdots
$$

Start with

$$
R^a_{bcd} = \partial_c \Gamma^a_{bd} - \partial_d \Gamma^a_{bc} + \Gamma^a_{ec} \Gamma^e_{bd} - \Gamma^a_{ed} \Gamma^e_{bc}
$$

Lowering the first index:

$$
R_{abcd} = g_{ae} R^e_{bcd}
$$

It has the symmetries

$$
R_{abcd} = - R_{bacd} \\
R_{abcd} = - R_{abdc} \\
R_{abcd} = R_{cdab}
$$

Also,

$$
R_{a[bcd]} = 0
$$

In four dimension it has $20$ independent components.

Contract the first and third indices:

$$
R_{bd} = R^a_{bad}
$$

or

$$
R_{ab} = g^{cd} R_{cadb}
$$

This has $10$ independent components in four dimensions.

Now, we construct a tensor from the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature that has exactly the same index symmetries as Riemann, and subtract it

$$
C_{abcd} = R_{abcd} - [\text{ricci part}]
$$

We choose the Ricci part so that

$$
g^{ac} C_{abcd} = 0
$$

The Weyl tensor is the completely trace-free part of the Riemann tensor.

What kind of tensors can we construct from $R_{ab}$?

The natural building blocks are 

$$
g_{ac}R_{bd}, \qquad 
g_{ad} R_{bc}, \qquad 
g_{bc}R_{ad}, \qquad 
g_{bd} R_{ac}
$$

The appropriate antisymmetric combination is

$$
g_{a[c} R_{d]b} - g_{b[c} R_{d]a}
$$

Remember the antisymmetrization convention:

$$
A_{[cd]} = \frac{1}{2} (A_{cd} - A_{dc})
$$

Therefore,

$$
g_{a[c} R_{d]b} = \frac{1}{2} (g_{ac} R_{db} - g_{ad} R_{cb})
$$

$$
g_{b[c} R_{d]a} = \frac{1}{2} (g_{bc} R_{da} - g_{bd} R_{ca})
$$

If we subtract only the Ricci tensor contribution, its trace does not necessarily remove the entire trace of Riemann. Because the Ricci tensor itself has a trace

$$
R = g^{ab} R_{ab}
$$

So we also need a term constructed from

$$
R g_{ab}
$$

The only natural rank-4 object is

$$
g_{a[c} g_{d]b}
$$

Thus we try the general form

$$
C_{abcd} = R_{abcd} + A(g_{a[c} R_{d]b} - g_{b[c} R_{d]a}) + B R g_{a[c} g_{d]b}
$$

We demand that Weyl has zero trace

$$
g^{ac} C_{abcd} = 0
$$

Lets calculate each term individually. First,

$$
g^{ac} R_{abcd} = R_{bd}
$$

Now consider another term

$$
g^{ac} g_{a[c} R_{d]b} = \frac{1}{2} g^{ac} (g_{ac} R_{db} - g_{ad} R_{cb}) \\
= \frac{1}{2} (n R_{db} - R_{db}) \\
= \frac{n-1}{2} R_{db}
$$

$$
g^{ac} g_{b[c} R_{d]a} = \frac{1}{2} g^{ac} (g_{bc} R_{da} - g_{bd} R_{ca}) \\
= \frac{1}{2}(R_{db} - g_{bd} R)
$$

Therefore,

$$
g^{ac} (g_{a[c} R_{d]b} - g_{b[c} R_{d]a}) = \frac{n-1}{2} R_{db} + \frac{1}{2}(R_{db} - g_{bd} R)
$$

$$
= \frac{n-2}{2} R_{db} + \frac{1}{2}g_{bd}R
$$

Now contract the scalar-curvature term,

$$
g^{ac} R g_{a[c} g_{d]b} = \frac{1}{2} R g^{ac} (g_{ac} g_{db} - g_{ad} g_{cb}) \\
= \frac{1}{2} R(n g_{db} - g_{db}) = \frac{n-1}{2} R g_{bd}
$$

Put everything together,

$$
0 = R_{bd} + A (\frac{n-2}{2} R_{db} + \frac{1}{2}g_{bd}R) + B (\frac{n-1}{2} R g_{bd})
$$

Group the $R_{bd}$ terms:

$$
1 + \frac{A(n-2)}{2} = 0
$$,

therefore,

$$
A = - \frac{2}{n-2}
$$

Now group scalar terms

$$
A \frac{1}{2} + B \frac{n-1}{2} = 0
$$

Solving we get,

$$
B = \frac{2}{(n-1)(n-2)}
$$

Therefore,

$$
C_{abcd} = R_{abcd} - \frac{2}{n-2}(g_{a[c} R_{d]b} - g_{b[c} R_{d]a}) + \frac{2}{(n-1)(n-2)} R g_{a[c} g_{d]b}
$$

This is the general Weyl tensor in $n$ dimensions.

The Weyl tensor contains curvature information that cannot be obtained by contracting the Riemann tensor.

The particles can collectively move closer together or farther apart. The volume of the cloud changes. This is associated with Ricci curvature.

$$
\boxed{\text{Ricci} \to \text{volume/focusing effect}}
$$

Now imagine a spherical cloud becoming stretched in one direction and compressed in another. Its volume can remain approximately unchanged, but its shape changes. That's tidal deformation.

$$
\boxed{\text{Weyl} \to \text{tidal distortion/shape deformation}}
$$
