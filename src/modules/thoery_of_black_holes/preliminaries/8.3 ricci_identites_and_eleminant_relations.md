### The Ricci Identities and the eliminant relations

We already have,

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = - \gamma_{(a)(b)(c),(d)} + \gamma_{(a)(b)(d),(c)}\\ + \gamma_{(b)(a)(f)} [\gamma^{(f)}_{(c)(d)} - \gamma^{(f)}_{(d)(c)}] \\
+ \gamma_{(f)(a)(c)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(d)} - \gamma_{(f)(a)(d)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(c)}
$$

Considering the $(1313)$-component of the above relation and making use of the following relations

$$
R_{1212} = C_{1212} + R_{12} - \frac{1}{6} R;\space R_{1324} = C_{1324} + \frac{1}{12} R; \space R_{1234} = C_{1234};\\

R_{3434} = C_{3434} - R_{34} - \frac{1}{6} R; \space R_{1313} = C_{1313}; \space R_{2323} = C_{2323}; \\

R_{1314} = \frac{1}{2} R_{11}; \space R_{2324} = \frac{1}{2}R_{22}; \space R_{3132} = - \frac{1}{2} R_{33}; \\

R_{1213} = C_{1213} + \frac{1}{2}R_{13}; \space R_{1334} = C_{1334} + \frac{1}{2}R_{13}; \\

R_{1223} = C_{1223} - \frac{1}{2}R_{23}; \space R_{2334} = C_{2334} + \frac{1}{2}R_{23}
$$

we get,

$$
- \Psi_0 = C_{1313} = R_{1313} = \gamma_{133,1} - \gamma_{131,3} \\
+ \gamma_{133} (\gamma_{121} + \gamma_{431} - \gamma_{413} + \gamma_{431} + \gamma_{134}) \\
- \gamma_{131} (\gamma_{433} + \gamma_{123} - \gamma_{213} + \gamma_{231} + \gamma_{132}) \\
$$

or substituting for the directional derivatives and the spin coefficients their designated symbols, we obtain

$$
D \sigma - \delta \kappa = \sigma(3 \epsilon - \epsilon^* + \rho + \rho^*) + \kappa(\pi^* - \tau - 3 \beta - \alpha^*) + \Psi_0
$$

As we have noted in context of standard tetrad formalism, we can write down a total of 36 equations by considering various different components of the first equation above. But in Newman-Penrose formulation, it suffices to write set of $18$ equations.

$$
D \sigma - \delta \kappa
=
\sigma(3\epsilon-\epsilon^\ast+\rho+\rho^\ast)
+\kappa(\pi^\ast-\tau-3\beta-\alpha^\ast)
+\Psi_0
$$

$$
D \rho-\delta^\ast\kappa

\rho^2+\sigma\sigma^\ast
+\rho(\epsilon+\epsilon^\ast)
-\kappa\tau^\ast
+\kappa(\pi-3\alpha-\beta^\ast)

$$

$$
D\tau-\Delta\kappa
=
\rho(\tau+\pi^\ast)
+\sigma(\tau^\ast+\pi)
+\tau(\epsilon-\epsilon^\ast)
-\kappa(3\gamma+\gamma^\ast)
+\Psi_1+\Phi_{01}
$$

$$
D\alpha-\delta^\ast\epsilon

\alpha(\rho+\epsilon^\ast-2\epsilon)
+\beta\sigma^\ast
-\beta\epsilon^\ast

+\pi(\epsilon+\rho)

$$

$$
D\beta-\delta\epsilon

\sigma(\alpha+\pi)
+\beta(\rho^\ast-\epsilon^\ast)

+\epsilon(\pi^\ast-\alpha^\ast)
+\Psi_1
$$

$$
D\gamma-\Delta\epsilon

\alpha(\tau+\pi^\ast)
+\beta(\tau^\ast+\pi)
-\gamma(\epsilon+\epsilon^\ast)
-\epsilon(\gamma+\gamma^\ast)
+\tau\pi

-\frac{1}{24}R
$$

D\lambda-\delta^\ast\pi

$$

$$
\Delta\lambda-\delta^\ast\nu

$$

$$
\delta\rho-\delta^\ast\sigma
=
\rho(\alpha^\ast+\beta)
+\sigma(\beta^\ast-3\alpha)

+\kappa(\mu-\mu^\ast)

$$

$$
\delta\alpha-\delta^\ast\beta

\mu\rho-\lambda\sigma

+\beta\beta^\ast
-2\alpha\beta

\delta\lambda-\delta^\ast\mu

\Delta\alpha-\delta^\ast\gamma

\nu(\rho+\epsilon)

-\Psi_3

**Notes**

Start with the Ricci identity in tetrad basis

$$
R_{(a)(b)(c)(d)} = - \gamma_{(a)(b)(c),(d)} + \gamma_{(a)(b)(d),(c)}\\ + \gamma_{(b)(a)(f)} [\gamma^{(f)}_{(c)(d)} - \gamma^{(f)}_{(d)(c)}] \\
+ \gamma_{(f)(a)(c)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(d)} - \gamma_{(f)(a)(d)} \gamma^{(f)}_{(b)(c)}
$$

This is essentially the ordinary formula for the Riemann tensor, but expressed in the tetrad basis rather than a coordinate basis.

What are $\gamma_{abc}$?

They are the Ricci rotation coefficients (also called connection coefficients in the tetrad frame).

The Newman–Penrose spin coefficients are particular combinations of these $\gamma$'s. Some combinations are:

$$
\kappa = \gamma_{311}; \space \rho = \gamma_{314}; \space \epsilon = \frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{211} + \gamma_{341});
$$

$$
\sigma = \gamma_{313}; \space \mu = \gamma_{243}; \space \gamma = \frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{212} + \gamma_{342});
$$

$$
\lambda = \gamma_{244},; \space \tau = \gamma_{312}; \space \alpha = \frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{214} + \gamma_{344});
$$

$$
\nu = \gamma_{242}, \space \pi = \gamma_{241}; \space \beta = \frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{213} + \gamma_{343})
$$

So the strategy is 

$$
\boxed{R_{abcd} \to \gamma_{abc} \to \text{spin coefficients}}
$$

and eventually we obtain equations involving

$$
\Psi_i, \Phi_{ij}, R
$$

and spin coefficients.

Now for ($1313$)-component

$$
R_{1313} = - \gamma_{131,3} + \gamma_{133,1} \\
+ \gamma_{31f} (\gamma^f_{13} - \gamma^f_{31}) \\
+ \gamma_{f11} \gamma^f_{33} - \gamma_{f13} \gamma^f_{31}
$$

Now after summing over $f=1,2,3,4$ and lowering the indices, we simplify and we get

$$
R_{1313} = \gamma_{133,1} - \gamma_{131,3} \\
+ \gamma_{133} (\gamma_{121} + \gamma_{431} - \gamma_{413} + \gamma_{431} + \gamma_{134}) \\
- \gamma_{131} (\gamma_{433} + \gamma_{123} - \gamma_{213} + \gamma_{231} + \gamma_{132}) \\
$$

Now, we know

$$
\Psi_0 = - C_{1313}
$$

And by one of the relations

$$
R_{1313} = C_{1313}
$$

We get,

$$
-\Psi_0 = \gamma_{133,1} - \gamma_{131,3} \\
+ \gamma_{133} (\gamma_{121} + \gamma_{431} - \gamma_{413} + \gamma_{431} + \gamma_{134}) \\
- \gamma_{131} (\gamma_{433} + \gamma_{123} - \gamma_{213} + \gamma_{231} + \gamma_{132}) \\
$$

The derivative part $\gamma_{133,1} - \gamma_{131,3}$ turns into the form

$$
D \sigma - \delta \kappa
$$

Therefore, we can simpliy to

$$
D \sigma - \delta \kappa = \sigma(3 \epsilon - \epsilon^* + \rho + \rho^*) + \kappa(\pi^* - \tau - 3 \beta - \alpha^*) + \Psi_0
$$

Similarly all other combinations can be derived in a similar fashion.
