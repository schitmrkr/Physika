### A generalized version of the tetrad formalism

We have explicitly assumed that $\eta_{(a)(b)}$ as defined by

$$
\eta_{(a)(b)} = e^i_{(a)} e_{i(b)}
$$

represents a constant symmetric matrix. If this should not be the case and the $\eta$ are functions in space time, then we cannot infer the antisymmetry of the rotation coefficients in their first two indices; instead, we shall have

$$
\gamma_{(a)(b)(c)} - \gamma_{(b)(a)(c)} = \eta_{(a)(b),(c)}
$$

The process of raising and lowering of tetrad indices by $\eta$ does not permute with the operation of directional or intrinsic differentiation.
