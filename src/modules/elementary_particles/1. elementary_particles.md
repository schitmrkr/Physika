### Elementary particles

**Photon**

Quantization of radiation

$$
E = h \nu
$$

**Meson**

The protons and neutrons must be attracted to each other by some field. The quantization of this field is Meson. It was supposed to be 300 times heavier than electron and 6 times lighter than proton. So, it is called Meson meaning "middle weight". Similarly, Lepton means "lighter weight" which implies electron. And Baryons  meaning "heavy weight" are proton and neutron. 

**Antiparticles**

Every particle has a corresponding antiparticle with the same mass but opposite charge and opposite values of relevant quantum numbers. Dirac's relativistic equation for the electron naturally produced both

$$
E = + \sqrt{p^2c^2+m^2c^4}
$$

and 

$$
E = - \sqrt{p^2c^2+m^2c^4}
$$

Originally, Dirac interpreted the negative-energy solutions using his "electron sea": all negative-energy states were filled, and a missing electron—a hole—would behave like a positively charged particle.

That hole was eventually understood to be the positron, discovered by Carl Anderson in 1932.

Modern quantum field theory gives a cleaner interpretation: we don't need an infinite sea of electrons. The negative-energy solutions can be reinterpreted as positive-energy states of the antiparticle.
