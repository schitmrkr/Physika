### Eigenfunctions

First of all we need to rewrite $L_x$, $L_y$ and $L_z$ in spherical coordinates. $\mathbf{L} = -i\hbar (\mathbf{r} \times \nabla)$ and the gradiant in spherical coordinates is,

$$
\nabla = \hat{r} \frac{\partial}{\partial r} + \hat{\theta} \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} + \hat{\phi} \frac{1}{r \sin \theta} \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi}
$$

meanwhile, $\mathbf{r} = r \hat{r}$, so

$$
\mathbf{L} = -i\hbar [r (\hat{r} \times \hat{r}) \frac{\partial}{\partial r} + (\hat{r} \times \hat{\theta}) \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} + (\hat{r} \times \hat{\phi}) \frac{1}{\sin \theta} \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi}]
$$

$$
\mathbf{L} = -i\hbar [\hat{\phi} \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} + \hat{\theta} \frac{1}{\sin \theta} \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi}]
$$

Now when we resolve for $L_z$,

$$
L_z = -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi}
$$

So, if $f$ is an eigenfunction of $L_z$,

$$
L_z f = -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial \phi} f = \lambda f
$$

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial \phi} = \frac{i \lambda}{\hbar} f
$$

$$
f(\phi) = A e^{i\lambda \phi/\hbar}
$$

Now we know that 

$$
\phi = 2 \pi + \phi
$$

So,

$$
A e^{i\lambda \phi/\hbar} = A e^{i\lambda (\phi + 2\pi)/\hbar}
$$

$$
e^{i \lambda 2 \pi / \hbar} = 1
$$

Therefore, 

$$
\lambda = m \hbar, \space m \in Z
$$

**What is $l$?**

Now we look for eigen functions that satisfy, 

$$
L^2 Y = \lambda_L Y
$$

$$
L_z Y = m \hbar Y
$$

The remarkable result is that

$$
L^2 Y^m_l = \hbar^2 l(l+1) Y^m_l
$$

$$
L_z Y^m_l = m \hbar Y^m_l
$$

So, 

$$
L^2 \to \hbar^2 l(l+1)
$$

Here from previous derivations we know that $l = 0,1,2,...$. So for $m$.

$$
|\mathbf{L}| = \sqrt{l(l+1)} \hbar
$$

Meanwhile,

$$
L_z = m \hbar
$$

Also,

$$
|L_z| \le |\mathbf{L}|
$$

So roughly,

$$
m \hbar \le \sqrt{l(l+1)} \hbar
$$
