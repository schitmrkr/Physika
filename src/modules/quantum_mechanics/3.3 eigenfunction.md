### Eigenfunctions of a Hermitian operator

The collection of all eigenvalues of an operator is called its spectrum. If the spectrum is discrete then the eigenfunctions lie in Hilbert space and they constitute physically realizable states. If the spectrum is continuous then the eigenfuntions are not normalizable, they do not represent possible wave function.

### Discrete spectra

Theorem 1: The normalizable eigenfunctions of a hermitian operator have real eigenvalues.

Proof: Suppose,

$$
\hat{Q}f = qf
$$

and

$$
\langle f|\hat{Q}f \rangle = \langle \hat{Q}f|f \rangle
$$

i.e. $\hat{Q}$ is hermitian. Then,

$$
q \langle f|f \rangle = q^* \langle f|f \rangle
$$

Since, $q = q^*$, $q$ has to be real.

Theorem 2: Eigenfunctions belonging to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.

Proof: Suppose,

$$
\hat{Q}f = qf, \space \hat{Q}g = q'g
$$

and $\hat{Q}$ is a hermitian. Then,

$$
\langle f|\hat{Q}g \rangle = \langle \hat{Q}f|g \rangle \\
q'\langle f|g \rangle = q^* \langle f|g \rangle
$$

Since, $q' \ne q$, then $\langle f|g \rangle = 0$.
