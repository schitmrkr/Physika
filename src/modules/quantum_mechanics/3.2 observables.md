### OBSERVABLES

### Hermitian Operators

The expectation value of an observables $Q(x, p)$ can be expressed very neatly in the inner product notation.

$$
\langle Q \rangle = \int \Psi^* \hat{Q} \Psi dx = \langle \Psi, \hat{Q} \Psi \rangle
$$

The outcome of a mesurement has to be real,

$$
\langle Q \rangle = \langle Q \rangle^*
$$

So,

$$
\langle \Psi, \hat{Q} \Psi \rangle = \langle \hat{Q} \Psi, \Psi \rangle
$$

We call such operators hermitians. It has even more stronger condition than this: 

$$
\langle f, \hat{Q} g \rangle = \langle \hat{Q} f, g \rangle
$$

**Is momentum operator hermitian?**

$$
\langle f, \hat{p} g \rangle = \int_{-\infin}^{+\infin} f^* (-i \hbar) \frac{dg}{dx} dx
$$

$$
= -i \hbar f^* g |_{-\infin}^{+\infin} + \int_{-\infin}^{+\infin} (-i \hbar \frac{df}{dx})^* gdx = \langle \hat{p} f, g \rangle
$$

### Determinant States

The standard deviation of $Q$, in a determinate state, would be zero,

$$
\sigma^2 = \langle (Q - \langle Q \rangle)^2 \rangle = \langle \Psi | (\hat{Q} - q)^2 \Psi \rangle = \langle (\hat{Q} - q) \Psi | (\hat{Q} - q) \Psi \rangle = 0
$$

Which means that

$$
\hat{Q} \Psi = q \Psi
$$

Determinate states of $Q$ are eigenfunctions of $\hat{Q}$. 

Determinate states of the total energy are eigenfunctions of the Hamiltonian.

$$
\hat{H} \psi = E \psi
$$
