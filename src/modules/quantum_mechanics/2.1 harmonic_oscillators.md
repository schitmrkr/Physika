### The harmonic oscillator

The quantum problem is to solve Schrodinger's equation for potential

$$
V(x) = \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2
$$

Thus the time independent schrodinger's equation becomes

$$
- \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2 \psi = E \psi
$$

#### Algebraic method

We write this in more suggestive form:

$$
\frac{1}{2m}[\hat{p}^2 + (m \omega x)^2] \psi = E \psi
$$

where, $\hat{p} = -i \hbar d/dx$ is the momentum operator. The basic idea is to factor the hamiltonian

$$
\hat{H} = \frac{1}{2m} [\hat{p}^2 + (m \omega x)^2]
$$

If they were numbers, it would have been easier. 

$$
u^2 + v^2 = (iu+v)(-iu+v)
$$

But since $\hat{p}$ and $x$ are operators, and they do not, in general, commute. Nonetheless, we examine these quantities.

$$
\hat{a}_\pm = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \hbar m \omega}} (\mp i\hat{p} + m \omega x)
$$

The factor infront is to make result look nicer.

Now consider the product $a_{-} a_{+}$,

$$
a_{-} a_{+} = \frac{1}{2 \hbar m \omega}(i \hat{p} + m \omega x) (-i \hat{p} + m \omega x)
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{2 \hbar m \omega}(\hat{p}^2 + (m \omega x)^2 - im\omega (x \hat{p} - \hat{p}x))
$$

The extra term $(x \hat{p} - \hat{p}x)$ is called the commutator of $x$ and $\hat{p}$. In general, the commutator of operator $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{B}$ is written as:

$$
[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] = \hat{A} \hat{B} - \hat{B} \hat{A}
$$

Thus,

$$
a_{-} a_{+} = \frac{1}{2 \hbar m \omega}[\hat{p}^2 + (m \omega x)^2] - \frac{1}{2 \hbar} [x, \hat{p}]
$$

Now, the commutator operates on function as follows:

$$
[x, \hat{p}]f(x) = [x(-i\hbar)\frac{d}{dx}(f) - (-i\hbar)\frac{d}{dx}(xf)] = i \hbar f(x)
$$

Thus,

$$
[x, \hat{p}] = i \hbar
$$

This ubiquitous formula is known as the canonical commutation relation.

Thus,

$$
a_{-} a_{+} = \frac{1}{2 \hbar m \omega}[\hat{p}^2 + (m \omega x)^2] - \frac{1}{2 \hbar} [x, \hat{p}]
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{\hbar \omega} \hat{H}  + \frac{1}{2}
$$

or,

$$
\hat{H} = \hbar \omega (a_{-} a_{+} - \frac{1}{2})
$$


Similarly,

$$
a_{+} a_{-} = \frac{1}{\hbar \omega} \hat{H}  - \frac{1}{2}
$$

In particular,

$$
[\hat{a}_- \hat{a}_+] = 1
$$


Hamiltonian can be written equally as

$$
\hat{H} = \hbar \omega (a_{+} a_{-} + \frac{1}{2})
$$

Thus the schrodingers equation for harmonic oscillator takes the form,

$$
\hbar \omega (\hat{a}_\pm \hat{a}_\mp \pm \frac{1}{2}) \psi = E \psi
$$


***
Claim: If $\psi$ satisfies schrodinger's equation with energy $E$ (that is, $\hat{H} \psi = E \psi$), then $a_{+}\psi$ satisfies equation with energy $(E + \hbar \omega): \hat{H}(\hat{a}_+ \psi) = (E + \hbar \omega) (\hat{a}_{+}\psi)$ .

Proof:

$$
\hat{H}(\hat{a}_+ \psi) = \hbar \omega (\hat{a}_{+} \hat{a}_{-} + \frac{1}{2}) (\hat{a}_+ \psi) = \hbar \omega (\hat{a}_{+} \hat{a}_{-} \hat{a}_{+} + \frac{1}{2} \hat{a}_{+}) \psi
$$

$$
= \hat{a}_+ [\hbar \omega (\hat{a}_-\hat{a}_+ + \frac{1}{2})] \psi = \hat{a}_+ [\hbar \omega (\hat{a}_+\hat{a}_1 + 1 +\frac{1}{2})] \psi 
$$

$$
= \hat{a}_+ (\hat{H} + \hbar \omega) \psi  = \hat{a}_+ (E + \hbar \omega) \psi = (E + \hbar \omega)(\hat{a}_+ \psi)
$$

Similarly,

$$
\hat{H}(\hat{a}_- \psi) = (E - \hbar \omega)(\hat{a}_- \psi)
$$


This can be interpreted as "ladder" of states for harmonic oscillators. 
***

If I keep applying the lowering operator, I might get state with energy less than 0 which is not allowed. We know that $\hat{a}_{-}\psi$ is a new solution to schrodinger's equation, but it might now be normalizable -- it might be zero or its square integral might be infinite. In practice, we have zero. There occurs a "lowest rung"($\psi_0$) such that:

$$
\hat{a}_{-}\psi_0 = 0
$$

so,

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \hbar m \omega}} (\hbar \frac{d}{dx} + m \omega x) \psi_0 = 0
$$

$$
\frac{d \psi_0}{dx} = - \frac{m \omega}{\hbar} x \psi_0
$$

This has a solution,

$$
\psi_0(x) = A e^{-\frac{m \omega}{2 \hbar} x^2}
$$

After normalization, we get 

$$
A^2 = \sqrt{m \omega/ \pi \hbar}
$$

Thus,

$$
\psi_0(x) = (m \omega/ \pi \hbar)^{1/4} e^{-\frac{m \omega}{2 \hbar} x^2}
$$

To determine the energy of this state we plug it into schrodinger equation. 

$$
\hbar \omega (\hat{a}_+ \hat{a}_- + 1/2) \psi_0 = E_0 \psi_0
$$

Since, $\hat{a}_- \psi_0 = 0$, we have,

$$
E_0 = \frac{1}{2} \hbar \omega
$$

To generate the excited state, we use the raising operator to increase the energy by $\hbar \omega$ with each step.

$$
\psi_n(x) = A_n (\hat{a}_+)^n \psi_0(x), \space E_n = (n + 1/2) \hbar \omega
$$

where $A_n$ is a normalization constant.
