### The Delta-Function Well

The Dirac delta function is defined as 

$$
\delta(x) = \begin{cases}
0, & x\ne 0,\\
\infin, & x = 0\\
\end{cases}, \space with \space\int^{+\infin}_{-\infin} \delta(x) dx = 1
$$

Lets consider a potential of the form,

$$
V(x) = - \alpha \delta(x)
$$

where $\alpha$ is some positive constant.

$$
 \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2} - \alpha \delta(x) \psi = E \psi
$$

it yeilds both bound state $(E \lt 0)$ and scattering state $(E \gt 0)$. 

We'll look at bound state first. In the region $x \lt 0$, $V(x) = 0$, so,

$$
\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2} = - \frac{2mE}{\hbar^2} \psi = \kappa^2 \psi
$$

The general solution to the equation is 

$$
\psi(x) = Ae^{-\kappa x} + Be^{\kappa x} 
$$

The first term blows up as $x \to - \infin$, so we choose $A=0$.

$$
\psi(x) = B e^{\kappa x}, (x \lt 0)
$$

In the region $x \gt 0, V(x) = 0$,

$$
\psi(x) = F e^{-\kappa x}, (x \gt 0)
$$

The boundary condition tells us that $B = F$

$$
\psi(x) = \begin{cases}
Be^{\kappa x}, & x \le 0,\\
Be^{- \kappa x}, & x \ge 0
\end{cases}
$$

The idea is to integrate schrodinger's equation from $-\epsilon$ to $+\epsilon$, and thne take limit as $\epsilon \to 0$

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \int^{+\epsilon}_{-\epsilon} \frac{d^2 \psi}{dx^2} dx + \int^{+\epsilon}_{-\epsilon} V(x) \psi(x) dx = E \int^{+\epsilon}_{-\epsilon} \psi(x) dx
$$

The first integral is nothing but $d\psi/dx$, evaluated at two end points; the last integral is zero in limit $\epsilon \to 0$. Since V(x) is a delta function.

$$
\Delta (\frac{d \psi}{dx}) = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} (\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}|_{+\epsilon} - \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}|_{-\epsilon}) = \frac{2m}{\hbar^2} \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \int^{+\epsilon}_{-\epsilon} V(x) \psi(x) dx
$$

$$
\Delta (\frac{d \psi}{dx}) = - \frac{2m \alpha}{\hbar^2}  \psi(0)
$$

For the case at hand,

$$
d\psi/dx = -B \kappa e^{-\kappa x}, \space (x \gt 0), \space d\psi/dx|_+ = -B \kappa
$$

$$
d\psi/dx = B \kappa e^{\kappa x}, \space (x \lt 0), \space d\psi/dx|_- = B \kappa
$$

hence, $\Delta(d\psi/dx) = -2B\kappa$ and $\psi(0) = B$, therefore,

$$
\kappa = \frac{m \alpha}{\hbar^2}
$$

The allowed energy

$$
E = - \frac{\hbar^2 \kappa^2}{2m} = - \frac{m \alpha^2}{2 \hbar^2}
$$

Finally as we normalize $\psi$, we get $B = \sqrt{\kappa} = \sqrt{m \alpha}/\hbar$

Evidently the delta function well, regardless of "strength" $\alpha$, has exactly one bound state:

$$
\psi(x) = \frac{\sqrt{m \alpha}}{\hbar} e^{-m \alpha |x| / \hbar^2}; \space E = - \frac{m \alpha^2}{2 \hbar^2}
$$

What about scattering state, with $E \gt 0$? For $x \lt 0$ the scrodinger equatin reads

$$
\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2} = - \frac{2mE}{\hbar^2} \psi = \kappa^2 \psi
$$

The general solution is 

$$
\psi(x) = A e^{ikx} + B e^{-ikx}
$$

Also at $x>0$,

$$
\psi(x) = F e^{ikx} + G e^{-ikx}
$$

Continuity of $\psi(x)$ at $x=0$ implies

$$
A + B = F + G
$$

The derivatives are:

$$
d\psi/dx = ik(F e^{ikx} - G e^{-ikx}), \space (x \gt 0), \space d\psi/dx|_+ = ik(F-G)
$$

$$
d\psi/dx = ik(A e^{ikx} - Be^{-ikx}), \space (x \lt 0), \space d\psi/dx|_- = ik(A-B)
$$

and hence $\Delta (d\psi/dx) = ik(F-G-A+B)$, and $\psi(0) = A+B$. Thus,

$$
ik(F-G-A+B) = -\frac{2m \alpha}{\hbar^2} (A+B)
$$
