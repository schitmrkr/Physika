### Symmetry and Conservation law

Suppose a physical quantity is represented by an operator

$$
\hat{A}
$$

It's expectation value in a state $|\psi(t) \rangle$ is

$$
\langle A \rangle = \langle \psi(t) | \hat{A} | \psi(t) \rangle
$$

We call $A$ conserved if

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle A \rangle = 0
$$

for every physical state.

#### Derivation of conservation condition

$$
\langle A \rangle = \langle \psi(t) | \hat{A} | \psi(t) \rangle
$$

Differentiate with respect to time

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle A \rangle = \frac{d \langle \psi(t) |}{dt} \hat{A} | \psi(t) \rangle + \langle \psi(t) | \frac{d\hat{A}}{dt} | \psi(t) \rangle + \langle \psi(t) | \hat{A} \frac{d| \psi(t) \rangle}{dt}
$$

Now use schrodinger's equation

$$
i \hbar \frac{d}{dt} | \psi \rangle = \hat{H} | \psi \rangle
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dt} | \psi \rangle = - \frac{i}{\hbar} \hat{H} | \psi \rangle
$$

Taking the hermitian conjugate

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle \psi | =  \frac{i}{\hbar} \hat{H} \langle \psi |
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle A \rangle = \frac{i}{\hbar} \langle \psi |\hat{H} \hat{A} - \hat{A} \hat{H}| \psi \rangle + \langle \frac{\partial \hat{A}}{\partial t} \rangle
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle A \rangle = \frac{i}{\hbar} \langle [\hat{H}, \hat{A}] \rangle + \langle \frac{\partial \hat{A}}{\partial t} \rangle
$$

This is Ehrenfest equation for an arbitrary operator.

If $\hat{A}$ has no explicit time dependence them,

$$
\frac{\partial \hat{A}}{\partial t} = 0
$$

then,

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle A \rangle = \frac{1}{i \hbar} \langle [\hat{A}, \hat{H}] \rangle
$$

Therefore, if $[\hat{A}, \hat{H}] = 0$, then


$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle A \rangle = 0
$$

So, $A$ is conserved.

#### Where does symmetry enter?

The operator $\hat{A}$ should commute with the Hamiltonian if there is symmetry. Suppose a transformation is represented by unitary operator $\hat{U}$. For example, a rotation can be represented by

$$
\hat{U}(R)
$$

If the system posses that symmetry, the physics must be unchanged under transformation. Mathematically, this means

$$
\hat{U} \hat{H} \hat{U}^\dagger = \hat{H}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
[\hat{U}, \hat{H}] = 0
$$

So a symmetry operator commutes with the Hamiltonian.

#### Continuous symmetries

Now suppose the symmetry depends continuously on a parameter $\epsilon$.

We can write

$$
\hat{U}(\epsilon) = e^{-i \epsilon \hat{G} / \hbar}
$$

where $\hat{G}$ is called generator of transformation. For example:

- Spatial translation - $\hat{P}$
- Rotation - $\hat{L}$
- Time translation - $\hat{H}$

The symmetry condition is 

$$
\hat{U}(\epsilon) \hat{H} \hat{U}^\dagger = \hat{H}
$$

Insert

$$
\hat{U}(\epsilon) = e^{-i \epsilon \hat{G} / \hbar}
$$

For an infinitesimal transformation, we use taylor expansion upto first order:

$$
\hat{U}(\epsilon) \simeq 1 - \frac{i \epsilon}{\hbar} \hat{G}
$$

Similarly

$$
\hat{U}^\dagger(\epsilon) \simeq 1 + \frac{i \epsilon}{\hbar} \hat{G}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\hat{U} \hat{H} \hat{U}^\dagger \simeq (1 + \frac{i \epsilon}{\hbar} \hat{G}) \hat{H} (1 - \frac{i \epsilon}{\hbar} \hat{G})
$$

$$
= \hat{H} + \frac{i \epsilon}{\hbar} [\hat{H}, \hat{G}]
$$

Symmetry requries this to be equal to $\hat{H}$:

$$
\hat{H} + \frac{i \epsilon}{\hbar} [\hat{H}, \hat{G}] = \hat{H}
$$

Therefore,

$$
[\hat{H}, \hat{G}] = 0 
$$

And from our conservation theorem,

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \langle \hat{G} \rangle = 0  
$$

Hence continuous symmetry implies conserved generator.

#### Spatial Translation (Momentum Conservation)

Consider a translation

$$
\mathbf{r} \to \mathbf{r} + \mathbf{a}
$$

The unitary transformation is

$$
\hat{U}(\mathbf{a}) = e^{-i \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{P}} / \hbar} 
$$

So the generator is $\mathbf{\hat{P}}$. Now the hamiltonian is 

$$
\hat{H} = \frac{\mathbf{\hat{P}}}{2m} + V(\mathbf{r})
$$

For a system to invariant under arbitrary translation, we need

$$
V(\mathbf{r}) = V(\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{a})
$$

Then,

$$
[\hat{P}, \hat{H}] = 0
$$

So, translation symmetry imples momentum conservation.

#### Rotational Symmetry (Angular Momentum Conservation)

A rotation is generated by angular momentum

$$
\hat{U}(\theta) = e^{-i \theta \cdot \hat{L} / \hbar}
$$

Rotational symmetry gives

$$
[\hat{L}, \hat{H}] = 0
$$

#### Time Translation (Energy Conservation)

Time evolution itself is generated by hamiltonian:

$$
| \psi(t) \rangle = e^{-i \hat{H} t / \hbar} | \psi(0) \rangle
$$

If the Hamiltonian has no explicit time dependence, then

$$
\frac{\partial \hat{H}}{\partial t} = 0
$$

then,

$$
\frac{d}{dt} \hat{H} = \frac{1}{i\hbar} [\hat{H}, \hat{H}] = 0
$$

Thus time translation symmetry implies energy conservation.
